# TikTok Production Capacity Planning for a Solo Creator

Build your TikTok content calendar around the time you have to make and review the videos. For a solo creator selling one digital product, production shares time with product updates, customer questions, and everything else required to keep the business running.

Start capacity planning with available work time, then account for preparation, recording, review, and rework. Use those estimates to set the maximum number of videos you can finish. You can choose to publish fewer videos if the useful ideas or required evidence are not ready.

This worksheet supports the broader [TikTok workflow for an owned product](https://groniz.com/blog/how-to-make-money-by-automating-your-tiktok/). Its numbers are hypothetical workload assumptions, not production benchmarks, recommended posting frequency, or revenue projections.

## Count the whole job

Leave time for the work after recording. You may still need to check a product claim, remove a private detail, repair an unreadable screen capture, verify the destination, and prepare the approved file for delivery.

Separate work you do once for the week's batch from work repeated for each video. Weekly topic selection may be shared. Checking whether a specific demonstration is accurate belongs to that video. Avoid putting the same task in both categories.

Set aside time for rework so that a task taking longer than expected does not force you to skip review. It can also absorb a failed recording or a product change that invalidates a planned demonstration.

If you have previous work records, use them to inform your estimates. If you do not, label the first budget provisional and record actual effort as you go. Write down which tasks each estimate includes; that will help you locate the source of an overrun.

## Build a weekly workload budget

The following example assumes a hypothetical creator has 300 minutes available for TikTok work during one week. This is an illustrative allocation chosen to demonstrate the calculation.

| Work category | Hypothetical minutes | What is included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Total available time | 300 | Time the creator can allocate after other commitments |
| Rework reserve | 60 | Retakes, corrections, and unexpected review work |
| Shared topic selection | 20 | Choose questions tied to the owned product |
| Shared weekly review | 25 | Review previous work and decide what to change |
| Shared delivery administration | 15 | Prepare the batch handoff and inspect delivery state |
| Time remaining for individual videos | 180 | Preparation, recording, editing, and approval |

Estimate the work you will repeat for each video:

| Per-video task | Hypothetical minutes | Completion condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Brief and evidence preparation | 15 | Task, claim, and sample inputs are ready |
| Recording and editing | 25 | The demonstration is readable and coherent |
| Fact, fit, and destination review | 15 | Claims checked and CTA route tested |
| Final file handoff | 5 | Approved asset and delivery details are ready |
| Total per video | 60 | All required work is accounted for |

Under these assumptions, the capacity ceiling is three videos: 180 available minutes divided by 60 minutes per video. The full allocation is 60 minutes of reserve, 60 minutes of shared work, and 180 minutes of individual production.

## Test the budget against a harder week

Suppose the same hypothetical creator plans more demanding screen demonstrations. Recording and editing now take an estimated 40 minutes per video instead of 25. Each video's total rises to 75 minutes.

The ceiling falls to two complete videos because three would require 225 minutes, exceeding the 180-minute production allocation. Two use 150 minutes, leaving 30 minutes unallocated within that production budget. Starting a third video would leave 45 minutes of work that the budget cannot cover.

The creator can choose simpler tasks, reduce the number of videos, or explicitly allocate more available time if their other commitments permit it. Keep evidence review in the estimate, even if it means making fewer videos.

A reusable formula is:

> Capacity ceiling = round down ((available time − rework reserve − shared work) ÷ estimated work per video).

Use the formula only when your videos have reasonably similar workloads. For a mixed week, estimate each item separately and add their costs until the next item would exceed the available budget.

## Keep a capacity worksheet beside the calendar

Use your own planning file to keep the budget and work records together:

- Record the hours or minutes you can commit this week.
- List each shared batch task and its time estimate.
- Set a rework reserve and say which tasks may use it.
- For each candidate video, note the buyer question, required evidence, estimated effort, and whether it is ready to make.
- Select the videos that fit the budget, including their review and handoff time.
- Log actual effort, including where estimates differed and why.
- Choose one change to next week's plan based on those records.

Use the [weekly TikTok content review](https://groniz.com/blog/weekly-tiktok-content-review-decide-what-to-keep-change-or-stop) to choose which questions deserve another video. Then place the selected work into a [single-product content calendar](https://groniz.com/blog/7-day-tiktok-content-calendar-for-a-single-product). Put preparation and review days on the calendar too. You can plan a full week without scheduling a video for every day.
